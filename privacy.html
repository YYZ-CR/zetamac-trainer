Privacy Policy

Last updated 28 July 2026

This is a small, free arithmetic trainer. It has no analytics, no tracking pixels, no advertising and no third-party trackers of any kind — there is no Google Analytics, Plausible, Umami, PostHog, Fathom or Sentry on any page. What follows is the whole list of what is stored, where it goes, and who else necessarily sees a request.

Playing without an account

You can play the timed game, use practice mode and accept somebody’s duel link without signing in. In that case nothing about you is stored against a person: the run is kept in your own browser, and the row the server holds for it has no owner.

Zetamac Daily, private clans, creating a duel and having a profile page all need an account, because each of them has to be able to tell one player from another.

If you make an account

Authentication runs on Supabase. An account is an email address and a password, and that is the only personal detail asked for. The password is handled by Supabase and is never seen by this site’s own code. Email confirmation may be switched on, in which case Supabase sends you a confirmation email; your address is used for that, for password resets, and for nothing else. There is no mailing list.

Stored against your account:

  • Your username, and the date you last changed it.
  • Whether your profile is public. Off by default.
  • Every game you finish — the score, the duration, the configuration you played, and the full question list: each question as it was displayed, the answer, whether you fumbled it, what you typed when you did, and how long it took you, to the millisecond. That per-question timing is the point of the site, and it is the most detailed thing here by a distance.
  • Daily attempts — the date, when you started and submitted, your score, and the answers that were accepted.
  • Duel runs — the same shape, plus which side you played and whether you were signed in or a guest.
  • Clan membership — which clans you are in, which you own, and when you joined.

What other people can see

Your profile page is private until you turn it on, in Settings. Until then /@yourname shows nothing to anybody but you. Turned on, it shows your username, the date you joined, your best scores, how many games and questions you have played, your accuracy, a per-operation breakdown of your average time, a percentile against other players, your recent scores and your streak. It never shows your email address, and it never shows an individual question.

Separately from that: if you play the daily, a clan board or a duel, the other people in it see your username and your score. That is what a leaderboard is. There is no way to appear on one anonymously.

A username is public wherever it appears, and it is not tied to your email address anywhere a visitor can reach.

What is kept in your browser

The site uses localStorage, not cookies, and every key it writes is functional rather than analytical. Nothing here is read by anyone else, and none of it leaves your device except the game data you choose to save:

  • zt_theme — which of the two themes you picked.
  • zt_tour_seen — that you have seen the walkthrough, so it does not open again.
  • pending_sessions, session_…, zt_last_session, zt_pending_game, zt_pending_session — finished and in-flight runs, so a game survives a refresh and so a run you played signed out can be claimed if you sign in later.
  • config_… — game configurations you have used or followed a link to.
  • practice_… — recent practice runs, which is how practice mode knows what you are worst at while signed out.
  • zt_daily_draft_…, zt_duel_draft_…, zt_duel_result_… — answers already given in a run in progress, and a finished duel result, so closing the tab mid-run does not lose it.
  • duel_guest_… — a random token that identifies your side of a duel you accepted without an account. It is not an identifier for you; it is an identifier for that one side of that one duel.

If you are signed in, the Supabase client also stores your login session in localStorage under a key beginning sb-. Clearing site data signs you out and loses any run that had not been saved to an account.

Who else sees a request

Three third parties are unavoidably involved in loading a page, and each of them therefore sees your IP address and the usual request headers:

  • Supabase — the database and the authentication service. Everything described under If you make an account is stored there.
  • Vercel — the host serving the pages.
  • jsDelivr — the CDN that serves the Supabase client library and Chart.js. It sees an IP address on every page load, before you have done anything at all. That is a real disclosure and most policies would not mention it; it is here because it is true. Nothing about your account or your scores is sent to it.

Each of them has its own privacy policy, and their logs are theirs rather than this site’s. No data is sold, and none is shared with anyone else.

Deleting your account

Deletion is self-service: Settings, at the bottom, in the Danger zone. You confirm by typing your own username (or the literal DELETE if you never set one), and it happens immediately and in one transaction. It cannot be undone.

What is deleted:

  • Every game session of yours, including their per-question timings. They are deleted rather than left ownerless, so your old runs stop counting toward everyone else’s percentile.
  • Every daily attempt. You come off every daily leaderboard, including past days.
  • Your clan memberships.
  • Duels you created, together with both players’ runs in them. This costs your opponent their result, and it is stated here rather than buried: a duel is the creator’s object and it is reachable only by its own link.
  • Your profile row, which releases your username for anyone else to take.
  • Your login, last of all — and with it Supabase’s own record of your identities, sessions and refresh tokens.

What survives, and why:

  • Duels you played as the opponent. The creator’s result is theirs and does not depend on you still having an account. Your run keeps its score and loses its owner, so you appear there as a deleted account rather than vanishing mid-comparison. If you had claimed a duel but never finished it, that side is released instead and the creator’s link works again.
  • Clans you own that still have members. Ownership is handed to the longest-standing remaining member, rather than the clan disappearing and taking everyone else’s board with it. A clan whose last member leaves is deleted.
  • Game configurations. The rows describing an operation mix and a duration are shared, keyed by their own contents, and name nobody — so they are not deleted.
  • Your browser’s localStorage. Deleting the account signs you out but does not reach into your device. Clear site data if you want the local copies gone too.

The full ordered contract is docs/account-deletion.md in the source, and it is what the code is built against.

Children

This is not aimed at children and asks for nothing beyond an email address. If a child has made an account and you would like it removed, deleting it needs no request — but write to the address below if you would rather it were handled for you.

Changes

If what is stored changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top moves. There is no notification mechanism, because there is no mailing list.
